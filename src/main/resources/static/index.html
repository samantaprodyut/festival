through cinema and music

Organised by the School of Journalism and Mass Communication, AURO University, AU-IFMF brings together students, independent creators, professionals and artists from across India and around the world. The festival showcases compelling stories, innovative ideas and original artistic expression, and encourages cultural exchange, creative collaboration and conversations that matter.

The host

AURO University

AURO University, Surat, was founded by the USA-based Rama family and draws its inspiration from the vision and teachings of Sri Aurobindo Ghosh and the Divine Mother. Its vision is to be a premier university of integral and transformational learning for future leaders, giving equal emphasis to the mental, physical, vital and spiritual dimensions of human development. The University is established under the Gujarat State Private Universities Act, 2009, and is recognised under Sections 2(f) and 22 of the UGC Act, 1956, and the Regulations, 2003. At its heart is the Sri Aurobindo Integral Life Centre, created as a source of inspiration and learning for self-development and humanity's evolutionary journey through integral and transformative education.

The University offers programmes across eight schools — Business, Design, Hospitality and Management, Information Technology, Integral Education, Journalism & Mass Communication, Law, and Liberal Arts & Human Sciences. Uniquely in the country, it hosts an international hotel brand, Courtyard by Marriott, operating on campus, bridging industry and academia with hands-on experience for students across all eight schools. Alongside these, AURO has established specialist centres in arts and creativity, language learning, rural management, innovation, incubation and entrepreneurship, family business, and legal aid.

The organisers

School of Journalism
& Mass Communication

The School of Journalism and Mass Communication (SoJMC) at AURO University, Surat, prepares students for careers across journalism, advertising, public relations, film and the wider media and entertainment industry, on a curriculum that balances editorial and communication fundamentals with hands-on production practice.

Teaching is deliberately practice-led. Students work across print, broadcast, digital and audio-visual formats — reporting and anchoring, camera and editing, design, campaign planning and brand storytelling — supported by studio and post-production facilities and by faculty drawn from both academia and industry. Every student is attached to a faculty mentor, and a structured placement pipeline takes them from resume building and grooming through mock interviews to corporate interviews and offers.

Programmes Offered

BA (Hons) Journalism & Mass Communication — an undergraduate honours programme covering media theory, reporting and writing, advertising and public relations, audio-visual and film production, digital media and media management, with practicals, internships and a portfolio requirement built into the structure.

MA Journalism & Mass Communication — a two-year postgraduate programme offering advanced specialisation, research and media project management for graduates seeking senior editorial, communication and production roles.

PhD in Journalism & Mass Communication — a doctoral programme for scholars pursuing original research in media, communication and allied areas, with supervision by the School’s faculty and a focus on work of academic and industry relevance.

The theme

One idea,
two channels

The inaugural edition runs under a single theme carried across both halves of the festival. Cinema and music are programmed together, in the same rooms, on the same days — because the audience for one has always been the audience for the other.

What happens

Five things going on
at once, for four days

01

Competition & screenings

Three film categories and five music and dance categories, judged across amateur and professional tracks. Selected films screen on campus in the Cinematic Spotlight blocks; finalists in music and dance perform live on the outdoor stage.

02

Fireside chats

Long-form conversations on the main stage and online — from building high-intensity cinema to the making of a singing voice, and the sound design an audience feels but never sees.

03

Workshops & masterclasses

Hands-on sessions in the AB3 rooms and the Student Centre: cinematography, colour, large-format cameras, mobile filmmaking, songwriting, bedroom production, live sound and scoring for the screen.

04

History Breathes

A documentary strand across three days on Lal Bahadur Shastri, R K Laxman, Anjaan and Vinda Karandikar, each screening followed by an interaction with family and subject experts.

05

The Proshow

The closing day builds from an afternoon stage workshop and a headline-artist conversation to a curtain raiser by the festival's own winners, then a full-scale evening concert.

Which track is mine? The track follows the director, not the crew. Your entry belongs in Track II if the director has had a film released theatrically, licensed to a broadcaster or streaming platform, or has been paid professionally to direct. Everything else is Track I. A student film made as coursework stays in Track I even where faculty or professionals helped. The same film cannot be entered in both tracks.

Who can enter, and how?These three categories are open to students and independent creators alike — no professional or amateur split applies here. Category 01 is restricted to entrants in India (A3, 50 entries). Categories 02 and 03 accept 60-second vertical reels from anywhere. There is no entry fee for the 2026 edition, and entries close on 6 September 2026. Every Reel and Poster entry is first assessed in a preliminary round on the basis of the details and files submitted with the entry form; only shortlisted entries move forward to final judging and the festival screening.
Note: To qualify for the selection process, participants must follow both @schoolofmasscomm.au and @confluence_auro on Instagram, upload their entry to their own account, and send a collaboration request to both pages. The post must stay public until the results are announced.

Questions

Everything else

What is the festival all about?

AU-IFMF is a four-day film and music festival on the AURO University campus in Surat, running 22–25 October 2026 under the theme Beyond Borders. It combines a competitive film festival, a live music and dance competition, and a programme of screenings, fireside chats, panel discussions, workshops and masterclasses, closing with a full-scale concert.

Who can participate?

Students, independent artists, music bands, dance groups, cultural teams, universities and colleges, and performing arts institutions. Film entries are open worldwide; music and dance finalists must be able to perform live on campus during the festival.

Is there an entry fee?

No. There is no entry fee for the 2026 edition, in any category, in either track.

How many entries can I submit?

Up to three film entries, with a maximum of one per category, and a separate entry form for each. Music and dance entries are per category and per group.

My film isn't in English. Can I still enter?

Yes — entries may be in any language. Work not in English must carry burnt-in English subtitles. The jury cannot assess an entry without them, so this is the single most common reason an otherwise strong entry is set aside.

How do I know whether I'm Amateur or Professional?

The track follows the director, not the crew. Track II applies if the director has had a film released theatrically, licensed to a broadcaster or streaming platform, or has been paid professionally to direct. Everything else is Track I, including student coursework made with faculty or professional help. The same film may not be entered in both tracks.

Do music and dance entrants perform live?

Every music and dance entry is first assessed through a video preliminary round submitted by the deadline. Finalists are announced with the official selections on 16 September and perform live on campus during the festival.

What equipment does the festival provide?

A full PA system and stage monitors, a drum kit with hardware, two guitar amplifiers and one bass amplifier, a keyboard stand, four vocal microphones and DI boxes. Bring your own guitars, bass, keyboards and synthesisers, preferred cymbals and snare, effects pedals and cables, and in-ear monitors if you use them.

Can I withdraw my entry?

Entries may not be withdrawn after official selections are announced on 16 September 2026.

What rights am I giving the festival?

A non-exclusive right to screen or perform your entry during the festival, and at up to two non-commercial campus or travelling showcases within twelve months. The festival may use an extract of up to 60 seconds, plus stills and production credits, for promotion, with credit to you. All other rights remain yours.

Who owns the material I submit?

You do. By submitting, you confirm you are the rights holder or hold written permission from the rights holder, and that all music, footage, images and other third-party material in the work has been cleared for festival exhibition. You indemnify AURO University against any claim arising from the material submitted.

Can I appeal a jury decision?

No. The decision of the jury is final and binding on all participants.

Who will be speaking and judging?

Jury members, guests of honour and session speakers are confirmed and published alongside the official selections on 16 September 2026.

How do I become a sponsor?

Partnerships run from Title Partner down to single-session and single-category associations, and can be built around a specific workshop, competition, award or the closing Proshow rather than a fixed tier. Write to the Sponsorship & Partnerships team and they will send the deck and a proposal shaped to what you want the association to do.

Where is the venue?

AURO University, Earthspace, Hazira Road, Surat 394510, Gujarat, India. Sessions run across the Discovery auditorium, the Auro Student Center, the Executive Classroom, the AB3 rooms, the outdoor main stage, and online.
